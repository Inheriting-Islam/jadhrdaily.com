A daily Arabic root جذر

Three letters. A whole family of words.

Arabic is not a pile of vocabulary to be memorised. Almost every word in it grows from a root of three consonants. Jadhr shows you a family of words that all hide the same three — you work out which — and then the root blooms into everything it built, ending at the āyah where it lives.

One email, written by a person, when there is something real to open. No drip sequence, no list sold to anyone.

The minute

Three moves, and you are done for the day.

No lives, no energy bar, no reason to come back twice. It ends where it should end.

01

Hunt

You are shown a family of words that look nothing alike and mean nearly the same thing. Three consonants run through all of them. Find what repeats.

02

Bloom

The root you found lights up inside every word it made. Patience, the patient one, endurance, the command to endure — one skeleton, wearing different clothes.

03

The āyah

Then the root goes home. You meet it where it actually lives — in the Qur'an, in Uthmani script, with the verse it belongs to. That is the payoff, and it is the reason the whole thing exists.

Why a root and not a word

A mechanic only Arabic can have.

English word games guess spellings. Arabic has something no other puzzle can borrow: a real, systematic skeleton underneath the vocabulary. Learn one root and you have not learned one word — you have been handed the key to a family of them, and to every other word built on the same pattern for the rest of your reading life.

That is also why it survives being copied. Anyone can clone a grid of tiles. The thing that makes Jadhr worth opening is the content underneath it — authored roots, real word families, and every verse matched against the Qur'anic corpus rather than picked from memory.

Written down before launch, so it can be held against us

What Jadhr will never sell you.

Every one of these is a normal, profitable thing that daily games do. They are ruled out in the product spec, not in a blog post, because a Qur'anic word should never be the thing standing between you and a payment screen.

  • Never an extra guess for money. The limit is the game. Selling relief from it is selling frustration.
  • Never a paywall on the Bloom. Lose the puzzle and you still learn the root. The teaching is not the prize.
  • Never a paid hint you need. Support that learning requires is part of learning.
  • Never streak repair, lives or energy. Miss a day and nothing was taken from you.
  • Never an ad beside a verse. No advertising anywhere near Qur'anic text. Not a banner, not an interstitial.
  • Never your data as the product. The Daily works on your device, and your record of what you know is yours.

If Jadhr ever earns, it earns from tools for teachers, classes and masājid — the people who want more than a minute a day — while the daily puzzle stays whole and free for everyone else.

Where it honestly is

Playable, unfinished, and not pretending otherwise.

The app is real and runs end to end — hunt, bloom, verse, recall — on a rotation of 44 roots. What is not done is the part that matters most: every verse and every transliteration must be checked by a person before launch. Right now the content is machine-matched against the Qur'anic corpus and marked draft in the data itself, and nothing ships until it passes review. Reverence over speed.

Be first, honestly

It opens with a single email.

There is no launch sequence and no countdown. When the content has been checked and the thing is worth your minute, you get one note with a link in it. That is the whole plan.

Your browser will open your own mail app with the message already written — there is no form server here collecting addresses in the background. Send it and you are on the list.
